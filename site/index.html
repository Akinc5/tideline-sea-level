The forecast · LR · SVR · Random Forest

Machine learning that knows the physics

Warm water expands and warm air melts ice, so the rate of rise follows the warming. Rahmstorf and Vermeer wrote this as a law. Tideline gives the models its terms, the accumulated warming ∫T dt, and tests them on 2000–2024, which they never saw.

The semi-empirical law

dH/dt = a·(T − T₀) + b·dT/dt ⟹ H = a·∫T dt − a·T₀·t + b·T + c

Method

How Tideline works

Join the records

CSIRO tide-gauge reconstruction to 1992, NOAA satellite altimetry from 1993, PSMSL Mumbai, NASA GISTEMP; one annual table, 1880–2024.

Encode the physics

Cumulative warming ∫T dt, year and T are the terms of the Vermeer–Rahmstorf law, so Linear Regression on them is the published model.

Train three algorithms, three ways

LR, SVR and Random Forest on pure inputs, on physics features and as physics-guided hybrids, tuned with time-series cross-validation inside 1880–1999.

Test on the unseen future

MAE, MSE, RMSE and R² on 2000–2024, rolling-origin windows, and a shuffled split to show how it inflates scores.

Project and localise

Integrate the law under SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 warming, add Mumbai's land-motion offset, and check against IPCC AR6.

Simulate the defences

Gumbel storm surge, wave attenuation by mangroves, embankment overtopping, raised plinths, JRC depth-damage curves, expected annual damage and cost-benefit.

Model assumptions

Limitations

  • The town, its costs and the surge statistics are illustrative, not a survey of a real ward.
  • The flood model is a static bathtub with wave and surge attenuation; it does not route water inland over time.
  • The semi-empirical law assumes the past link between warming and rise continues, so it cannot represent sudden ice-sheet collapse.
